How much does it cost to start playing pickleball in India?

You do not need to buy a paddle before your first game. In one current Hyderabad example, four players sharing a one-hour court with paddles included works out to about ₹175–₹200 each. A basic own-equipment setup can start around ₹1,600 before court fees; a more complete regular-play setup can reach roughly ₹4,300–₹7,100.

Published 18 August 2026Prices checked 16–18 August 2026Examples, not a national average

Important

India does not have one pickleball price.

Court rates vary by city, venue, time slot, equipment inclusion and booking platform. We show dated examples and arithmetic instead of pretending there is a reliable national average.

The cheapest sensible way to start

Book or join a session where paddles are supplied, wear suitable court-safe shoes you already own, and play before purchasing equipment. Your first session should answer whether you actually want to keep playing.

What costs money?

A new player can encounter five separate costs:

  1. Court time — usually charged per court/session or through a membership/open-play format.
  2. Paddle — optional for the first session if the venue supplies or rents one.
  3. Balls — sometimes supplied, sometimes sold/rented separately.
  4. Shoes — you may already own appropriate court shoes; do not buy a specialist-labelled pair simply because the sport is new to you.
  5. Coaching or competition — optional, and highly variable.

Three realistic starter budgets

Budget A: “I just want to try pickleball once” — about ₹175–₹200 per person in one observed example

PicklePlex in Madhapur currently lists court pricing of ₹700 per hour in the morning including paddles and ₹800 per hour in the evening including paddles. Split between four players, that is ₹175–₹200 each for the hour before travel or optional add-ons.

The same listing quotes ₹50 for an optional tournament ball per booking. We are not treating PicklePlex as a national price benchmark; it is simply a current, transparent example of how cheap a first trial can be when equipment is included.

Pickleball Central in Peerzadiguda also currently advertises a promotional “first hour on us”. Promotions can change, so we would not build a national cost estimate around it—but it reinforces the practical point that buying equipment first is unnecessary.

Budget B: “I want my own basic paddle and balls” — from about ₹1,639 upfront

A current direct listing gives us a simple floor:

  • East India Pickleball Quantum Rally paddle: ₹999.
  • Hundred Delta P500 outdoor balls, pack of six: ₹640.

Total equipment outlay: ₹1,639, before shipping differences or a court booking.

If four players then split PicklePlex’s currently listed court-only rate—₹600 morning or ₹700 evening excluding paddles—the observed share is ₹150–₹175 each for an hour. That would put this example player’s first own-kit-plus-session spend around ₹1,789–₹1,814.

That is an example constructed from dated listings, not our claim that ₹1,800 is “the cost of pickleball in India”.

Budget C: “I expect to play regularly and want a more complete setup” — roughly ₹4,300–₹7,100 in our current example basket

For a player who wants a specification-documented paddle, a pack of balls and dedicated court shoes, current Indian listings support a broader basket:

  • Paddle: Hundred Spirit X at ₹1,990 through Zephyr 20 at ₹3,490 in current listings.
  • Outdoor balls: roughly ₹640–₹889 for current six-ball packs from Hundred.
  • Multi-court tennis shoes: Decathlon currently lists an entry TS100 at ₹1,499 and TS160 at ₹2,499.
  • One four-way court share in the PicklePlex court-only example: ₹150–₹175.

Using those observed products gives a first-session basket of roughly ₹4,279 at the low end to ₹7,053 at the high end. We round that to about ₹4,300–₹7,100 because exact checkout totals, sizes, promotions and delivery can change.

Do you need new shoes?

Not necessarily. If you already own stable tennis, badminton or other court shoes in good condition, use them for your first sessions. The purchase becomes relevant if your existing footwear is built for straight-line running, lacks lateral stability or is worn out.

We use tennis-shoe prices above because they are a current Indian court-footwear reference—not because a tennis shoe automatically performs better for every pickleball player. We have not physically tested those shoes for pickleball.

What about coaching?

We have deliberately left coaching out of the starter budgets. Public coaching prices are not consistent enough across the cities we cover to support a clean India-wide number, and group lessons, private sessions, academy packages and venue memberships are not comparable products.

When we have a broader dated coaching-price dataset, we can publish it. Until then, “₹X is the average coaching fee in India” would be false precision.

What about tournaments?

Competition is optional, but current Hyderabad examples show the next step does not necessarily require a huge budget. Pickleball Central recently listed a DUPR Night at ₹399 for singles and its August Season 5 tournament at singles from ₹799 and doubles from ₹1,500.

Those are one operator’s event prices, not a market average. Tournament fees depend on organiser, category, format, guarantees, venue and prize structure.

The recurring cost is more important than the first paddle

For somebody who plays twice a week, court access can overtake equipment cost quickly. A ₹2,000 paddle used for a year may be a smaller part of your pickleball spend than dozens of booked court hours.

That is why a good India-focused buying decision should ask:

  • What does my local court actually charge at the times I can play?
  • Can I split the court with three other players?
  • Does the venue include paddles or balls?
  • Is there cheaper open play, a membership or community session?
  • Will I need to travel significantly to reach the venue?

Why we are not publishing a national “average cost”

Our court directory spans multiple cities, but most venue records do not yet contain a directly verified current price. Booking platforms can show time-dependent prices that change by slot. Equipment promotions move frequently. Coaching products are not standardised.

A national average built from that evidence would look authoritative while being methodologically weak. We would rather show dated observations, source them, and expand the dataset until a defensible price index becomes possible.
