Pickleball kitchen rule, explained

You may stand in the kitchen and play a bounced ball. What you cannot do is volley while touching the kitchen or its line—or let the momentum from a volley carry you into it.

Updated 16 August 2026Non-volley-zone modelCheck event rules

The shortcut

A bounce changes the question.

If the ball has bounced, you may enter the non-volley zone to play it. If you hit it before it bounces, your contact with the zone and your momentum matter.

See common cases ↗
The important distinction

The kitchen is a non-volley zone, not a no-entry zone. A dink that bounces can be played from inside it. The restriction applies to a volley: striking the ball before it bounces.

What is the kitchen?

“Kitchen” is the informal name for the non-volley zone (NVZ), the area next to the net. Under the AIPA rulebook, it is 7 feet deep and 20 feet wide on each side of the net. The two zones together make the 14-foot area around the net often described in court diagrams.

The line marking the outer edge of the zone counts as part of the zone. For a volley, being on that line is the same as being in the kitchen.

The rule in one sentence

A player must initiate a volley from outside the non-volley zone. The formal rule language also covers a player, or anything in contact with the player, touching the zone during the act of volleying.

What counts as a volley?

A volley is a shot played before the ball bounces on the player’s side of the court. A ball that has bounced is a groundstroke. That distinction is why the rule does not stop players moving into the kitchen to retrieve a soft shot after it has bounced.

When may you step into the kitchen?

  • To play a bounced ball: yes. This is the ordinary response to a dink that lands in the zone.
  • While waiting for a bounced ball: yes. The zone is not forbidden space.
  • After a volley: only if your momentum from that volley does not carry you into the zone. The act of volleying has to be complete first.

Why momentum causes so much confusion

The rule is not satisfied merely because both feet were outside the line at the instant the paddle met the ball. If a player volleys from outside the zone and momentum from that volley carries the player, clothing, paddle or another item in contact with the player into the zone, it is a fault under the standard rule model. The rulebooks make clear that this can remain a fault even after the ball is dead.

In practical terms: after punching a high ball near the line, regain balance before allowing your body or anything you are carrying to touch the kitchen. A safe stance gives you room to stop, rather than relying on a close call.

Common kitchen situations

SituationWhat the standard rule model says
A dink lands in the kitchen and bouncesYou may step in and play it, because it is no longer a volley.
Your foot is on the kitchen line when you volleyFault. The line is part of the non-volley zone.
You volley outside the line, then fall forward into the kitchenFault if momentum from that volley carries you in, even if the rally has ended.
You are legally outside the kitchen and volley without entering itPermitted, subject to the rest of the rules.
You simply stand in the kitchen between shotsPermitted; being there is not itself a fault.

Three myths to leave behind

  1. “You can never enter the kitchen.” Incorrect. You may enter to play a ball after it bounces.
  2. “Only my feet matter.” Incorrect. The rule also addresses the player and items in contact with the player during the act of volleying.
  3. “Once the ball is dead, the volley cannot be a kitchen fault.” Incorrect under the current standard rule language: momentum from the volley can still create a fault.

Use the event’s current rulebook

Different organisers can publish their own regulations, formats or interpretations. This page explains the standard non-volley-zone framework reflected in the cited rule materials; it does not decide a disputed call at a particular Indian event. Before a tournament, read the current rules supplied by the organiser and ask the referee or tournament desk when a local procedure is unclear.
